Sources & credits

Ayah Grammar is a personal Quran grammar study project. Quran text, translations, word glosses, and morphology come from the named sources below. The verb chart shows forms found in the morphology corpus; it does not generate or present new Quranic words.

Arabic text and translations

  • Arabic Uthmani text, version 1.1: Tanzil Project. Copyright © Tanzil Project. The Quran text is displayed unchanged with source attribution; see the text license and updates.
  • English ayah translation: Saheeh International, distributed through Tanzil translations.
  • Bengali ayah translation: Muhiuddin Khan, distributed through Tanzil translations.
  • Surah names and ayah counts: Tanzil metadata.

Word meanings and grammar

  • English and Bengali word glosses: public word databases in the Greentech Apps Foundation Al Quran repository. These source databases retain their original ownership and source-specific reuse terms.
  • Word location, root, part of speech and verb features: Quranic Arabic Corpus morphology v0.4, copyright © 2011 Kais Dukes. The original copyright notice is retained with the source file and reproduced here. The Corpus documentation explains the tags used here.

Verb conjugation patterns

The “Full pattern” chart uses Qutrub 1.2.4.1 (GPL) to conjugate the placeholder roots فعل and فعلل. These are teaching patterns, not Quran words or conjugations of the selected verb. Form I uses one standard vowel pattern; weak roots can differ.

Bangla book conjugations

The “500 verbs” library and matching “Full conjugation” charts use Quran words — Level 1 (entries 1–200) and Level 2 (201–500), consulted from purchased personal copies. Thanks to the authors and publishers for this reference material. The original PDFs are kept locally and are not distributed with the repository or production app. The app uses extraction from the PDF glyphs, with source pages retained for checking individual entries.

There are 500 numbered entries and 6,978 supplied Arabic–Bangla cells. Entry 374 has two readings, both preserved. Entry 202's identical repeat is deduplicated. Entries 91 and 190 have no conjugation table; entry 30 has past forms only. The book supplies six masculine and first-person positions, with commands for the two masculine second-person positions. No missing feminine, dual or passive meanings have been generated.

Each chart retains its source-page reference for checking against a personal copy. Original-page links are available only in local development when the books are present. Numbering and completeness were checked, with targeted visual checks of text and anomalies; the whole collection has not been independently proofread. Obvious Bangla pronoun typos are marked and their original text retained in source notes. Known questionable printed Arabic forms are flagged for review. Glyph recovery normalizes spaces, Unicode, and the order of vowel marks, so the export is a readable transcription rather than a byte-for-byte PDF text copy.

The optional “all Arabic persons” view uses root-specific forms generated with Qutrub from a Corpus-matched citation. Bangla is attached only where root, form, voice, person, aspect and Arabic letters match a supplied book cell. Book meanings are study glosses, not contextual Quran translations. The “Quran examples” tab alone identifies attested occurrences with their contextual meanings.

A word-by-word gloss is a short contextual aid, not a replacement for a full translation. Grammar annotations can be corrected by their source projects. If a word, alignment, or label looks wrong, verify it against the linked source before relying on it.
